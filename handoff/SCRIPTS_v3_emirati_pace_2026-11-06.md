## Realism and science check (rules §1, done 6 Nov before voicing)
Realism: each script is AG talking to camera, not a scene with a patient; the lines are things a Gulf doctor-creator would say. The quiz, exam and POV framings are spoken as clear devices. Pass for all 13.
Science (claims unchanged from the 27 Oct approved scripts unless marked):
- Testosterone: 5 h sleep for 1 week lowers daytime testosterone by 10–15% in young men (Leproult & Van Cauter, JAMA 2011): "up to 15%" OK. Belly fat converts testosterone to estrogen via aromatase: OK. Age decline ~1% a year after 40: OK (cohort data, e.g. Massachusetts Male Aging Study). Obesity "can" lower it by about a third: OK as "ممكن". Zinc and vitamin D help only if deficient: OK. Heavy compound lifting helps, modest effect: OK as part of "free fix". CHANGED for accuracy: alcohol "ينزّله على طول" (drops it straight away) → "كل ما زاد نزّله" (the more you drink, the more it drops); the effect is dose-related.
- Magnesium: constipation (osmotic laxative) evidence clear; migraine prevention recommended in guidelines (AAN/AHS level B); sleep mixed, low-quality trials (50/50 is fair); cramps unlikely to help (Cochrane 2020) and anxiety evidence weak: OK. M2 "الدليل حقيقي" → "الدليل واضح" (same meaning).
- Whole-body MRI: not recommended for screening healthy people (ACR); incidental findings common and lead to more tests: OK.
- Peptides: insulin and semaglutide are peptides with evidence for specific uses; BPC-157 human evidence scarce, purity/safety uncertain, "research use only" products: OK.
- T6 fitness: fit older men can have better sexual function than sedentary young men; testosterone linked to waist size more than age; fitness (VO2 max) linked to erectile function: OK, worded with "ممكن".
